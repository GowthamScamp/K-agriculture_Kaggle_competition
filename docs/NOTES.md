# NOTES.md — running log of what was tried, what it scored, what to try next

All numbers are mean final bank over seeded episodes, with the paired 95% CI on
the per-seed margin. Single-episode figures are labelled as such and mean nothing
on their own — the seed-to-seed spread is roughly ±7,000.

---

## Phase 1 — Environment discovery

**The single most important finding of the whole project:** the competition ships
its environment publicly, inside `kaggle-environments` (1.32.7 on PyPI includes
`kaggriculture`). That means `interpreter()` — the real turn-update function — is
importable, so Phase 3 became *reuse* rather than *approximate*, and simulator
fidelity is exact rather than something to calibrate. This removed the largest
risk in the plan before any strategy code was written.

`docs/ENVIRONMENT.md` is generated by `python -m tools.discover_env`, not written
by hand, so it can be regenerated after any upstream change and diffed.

Deliverable check: do-nothing agent completes 720 turns and ends on exactly 3,000
(its starting money). The built-in `random` agent ends on **0** — it buys seeds it
never harvests. Activity is not profit.

### Three places `task.md`'s model of the game was wrong

1. **It is a spatial grid game.** A farmer avatar moves *one orthogonal tile per
   turn* and takes *one action per turn*. "Harvest and sell the wheat" is four
   decisions, not one. Every heuristic needs pathfinding.
2. **The action is compound**: one farmer op + one op per hired hand + up to ten
   market orders, every turn. The joint space cannot be enumerated, which is why
   Phase 4 searches macro-actions.
3. **Phase 3's preferred route was available**, so the approximate model and its
   calibration were never needed.

## Phase 2 — Rule-based baseline

Built, then fixed three times against measured evidence. The progression on seed 1:

| version | bank | defect found by measurement |
|---|---:|---|
| v1 | 11,963 | hired almost no hands; 62 of 75 tiles ended as weeds; **76% of unit-turns were walking** |
| v2 | 29,541 | `PLACE` consumed the animal from the *unit's* inventory, but `BUY_ANIMAL` deposits to the **shed** — the missing `PICKUP` hop meant 93% of unit-turns were a silent no-op |
| v3 | 55,985 | units picked up feed wheat then immediately dropped it (383 pickups, 76 feeds); crops valued at spot instead of harvest-day price |

Final: **44,293–47,678** mean over 10–16 seeds vs `starter`, 100% win rate,
t ≈ 17–25.

### What actually drives the score, in order

1. **Labour.** The n-th hire of a day costs `fib(n)` and the counter resets
   daily, so ten hands cost 143 coins *total* and each is a full extra action
   every turn. Fixing hiring was worth more than every market heuristic combined.
2. **Never plant what you cannot water.** `_new_plant` sets
   `consecutive_unwatered = 1`, so a crop must be watered *on its planting day*
   and every day after or it becomes a weed that blocks the tile. Over-planting
   is strictly worse than under-planting: it wastes the seed, the action, and
   then the ground. Planting is capped by watering capacity, not by land.
3. **Prices rise all season.** Town demand drains market inventory, so
   strawberry went 120 → 316 by day 25 in an early run. Valuing a harvest at
   today's price systematically underrates long-cycle crops; `projected_price`
   fixes this and it changed crop selection materially.
4. **Animals beat crops per action.** A cow needs one `FEED` plus an occasional
   `HARVEST` and returns ~1,760; a wheat tile needs watering every single day.

### Market facts worth remembering

- **MELON has no shop demand** — the town centre is its only buyer (1/day) and
  its glut curve is quadratic (`sq`, target 3.6). Dumping ~150 melons takes the
  price from 250 to the floor *permanently*. Acreage is capped for this reason.
- **WHEAT barely gluts** (`log`, target 0.2) and appears in 5 of 8 shop types, so
  it is the safe overflow crop and the animal feed.
- Each unit of a multi-unit `SELL` is re-quoted at the updated inventory, so a
  big sale walks its own price down. Sales are sized by marginal price.
- The shed holds 100 non-seed items and **end-of-day overflow is discarded**.
- Unsold goods score zero, hence the final-days liquidation.

### A bug class worth calling out

`type(raw) is dict` returned **False** for the environment's `Struct` tiles (a
`dict` *subclass*), so the fast policy classified every real tile as locked and
paved the farm with pastures. It never raised — it just played terribly. Both
this and the `PLACE` bug were invisible without an action census;
`tools/diagnose.py` exists because of them. `isinstance` everywhere now, with a
regression test.

## Phase 3 — Forward simulator

Reuses `interpreter()` directly. **Fidelity is exact over all 719 steps** of a
replayed episode; `tests/test_fidelity.py` asserts exact equality rather than a
tolerance and fails loudly if that ever stops holding.

Throughput was the real work. Measured, before → after:

| operation | before | after | note |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| `clone_state` | 691 µs | **25.8 µs** | `structify` recursively re-wrapped all 200 board tiles; replaced with a flat `Node` |
| `copy.deepcopy` (baseline) | 1,126 µs | 237 µs | for comparison — our clone is ~9× faster |
| `simulate_step` | 117 µs | **43.5 µs** | |
| `BaselineAgent` decision | 750 µs | **453 µs** | one cached tile classification instead of six passes |

> **Caveat on absolute timings.** Machine load moves all of these together.
> Three measurements of the same code:
>
> | condition | clone | step | step + policy |
> |---|---:|---:|---:|
> | idle | 25.8 us | 43.5 us | 1,503 us |
> | typical | 49.4 us | 67.9 us | 2,500 us |
> | under load | 65.5 us | 106.8 us | 3,379 us |
>
> Roughly a 2.5x spread, with the ratios between operations unchanged. The search
> adapts - it measures its own rollout cost at runtime and cuts the candidate
> list accordingly - but treat the idle figures as best-case and the loaded ones
> as the planning envelope.

**The binding constraint is the 1-second `actTimeout`.** At these speeds a
three-day rollout driving both seats costs ~65 ms, so a 450 ms budget buys
roughly ten rollouts per decision — which is why search runs once per in-game day
rather than every turn.

## Phase 4 — Macro-action search

Searches over whole-day strategies (crop, hand target, animal, expansion, market
posture), each executed by the Phase 2 agent, evaluated by rolling the exact
simulator forward two days and scoring shaped net worth.

**It is a one-ply evaluation, not a UCT tree, and that is deliberate.** The
environment is deterministic given both players' actions — weeds and shop
unlocks come from `Random(seed * 1_000_003 ^ day)`. With a fixed opponent a
rollout returns the same number every time, so repeated visits to a node buy
nothing. Budget goes to breadth instead.

Three fixes that mattered:

- **Rollouts must be interruptible.** Checking the clock only *between* rollouts
  let one late-season rollout push a turn to **1,191 ms** — over the limit, which
  forfeits the episode. The horizon is now also cut from inside the loop.
- **Partial sweeps are discarded.** If a sweep is cut short, some candidates were
  scored against a tough opponent and others against a soft one; the ranking then
  reflects the draw rather than merit.
- **Candidates are sized to the budget.** Proposing more options than can be
  scored meant scoring none and silently falling back to the default.

Valuation matters as much as search: pricing a standing crop at its *seed cost*
made every investment look like a loss inside a two-day window, and the search
chose `hands=0`. Pricing expected harvest instead fixed it.

## Phase 5 - Determinization

Implemented and working: samples K opponents (cheap policies spanning a range of
market aggression), scores every candidate against each, aggregates by `mean` or
`worst` via config. `src/replay_loader.py` is wired so real replays take over
automatically once `kaggle.json` exists - see `docs/REPLAYS.md`. With no replays
present it returns an empty list and the synthetic pool takes over, which is the
documented behaviour and is exercised directly.

**A 20-episode run said this lost to plain search. It does not.** See below.

### Findings

1. **Search beats the heuristic**, by ~8,900 coins (t=10.6). This held at n=20
   (+8,825, t=4.9) and holds at n=100 (+8,903, t=10.6). It is the one result
   strong enough to act on.

2. **The earlier "determinization loses" verdict was wrong, and n=20 is why.**
   The 20-episode run put det-mean at 37,116 against mcts at 41,044 and the
   reasoning written up around it - that splitting the budget across two
   opponent models costs more breadth than it buys robustness - was a *story
   fitted to noise*. At n=100 both aggregation rules land slightly **above**
   plain search. The correct statement is that determinization and plain search
   are **statistically indistinguishable** here: every head-to-head CI includes
   zero. Nothing has been shown to beat `mcts`, so `mcts` stays shipped - but it
   was not shown to be better either.

3. **Mean vs worst-case aggregation is still unresolved.** The paired gap is
   +540, CI [-280, 1,359], per-seed sd ~4,100.

   > **Superseded 23 Aug 2026.** This finding originally estimated that ~450
   > episodes would settle the question. That estimate assumed seed-to-seed
   > variation was the only noise. It is not: the same agent re-run on the same
   > seeds flips 18-22 of 100 outcomes, which the pairing does *not* cancel. More
   > episodes is the expensive way to fix this; a deterministic search budget is
   > the cheap one. See the 23 Aug section.

4. **Benchmark parallelism distorts search agents.** The budget is wall-clock, so
   running many episodes at once starves each decision. Same 100 seeds, same
   agent, only the worker count changed:

   | workers | mean bank | paired margin | 95% CI | max turn |
   |---:|---:|---:|---|---:|
   | 8 | 41,204 | +8,761 | [7,014, 10,507] | 506 ms |
   | 21 | 40,355 | +7,729 | [6,211, 9,246] | **821 ms** |

   Over-subscribing 22 cores costs ~1,000 coins of measured margin - though
   only on *margin*: at 84% the w21 win rate sits inside the 82-86% band that
   three identical w8 runs produced, so starving the search costs coins without
   often flipping a result. It does not
   mean the agent is near the timeout: on a quiet single process the slowest turn
   is **488 ms**, and a turn doing 0.6 ms of real work still spiked to 63.7 ms,
   which is OS scheduling latency landing in a wall-clock measurement. Benchmark
   search agents at `--workers 8` or fewer and read the timeout margin off a
   single-process run.

## What I would do next, in priority order

> **Rewritten 30 Aug 2026.** The old item 0 - "make the search budget
> deterministic" - is **done**: the candidate list no longer depends on machine
> speed. Doing it immediately exposed that the old items 1-2 were built on
> invalid measurements, so the list below is re-derived rather than re-ordered.

> **Updated 31 Aug 2026.** Item 1's *valuation* half is done and measured - it
> works (+54 points at 5 candidates) but does not beat the shipped config, so it
> ships as a flag defaulted off. The *horizon* half, item 4, is now the single
> remaining defect and has been promoted accordingly.

1. **Lengthen the rollout horizon.** `rollout_days=2` in a 30-day season. Proven
   to be a defect independent of valuation: `max_candidates=6` stays at 2% even
   with liquidation pricing, because no valuation accuracy lets a 2-day window
   see the cost of not selling. This is the only path left to making broader
   search useful - and it would likely make `valuation="liquidation"` worth
   shipping too, since the two interact. Everything below is downstream of it.

2. ~~**Fix the evaluator's valuation.**~~ **Done 31 Aug** - see the fifth pass.
   Kept here only to record that this is no longer the whole ballgame. `_value` scores a **2-day** rollout in a **30-day** season
   using `fast_net_worth`, which counts unsold inventory as wealth - so hoarding
   always looks profitable inside the window and is worth exactly zero at season
   end. That is why win rate *falls* monotonically past 3 candidates (93% -> 89%
   -> 77% -> 30% -> 1%) and why `sell-patient` loses 99 of 100.

   Two cheap probes, in order:
   - `shaped_reward=False` (score raw bank). Started, killed before finishing;
     it is one command and it tells you whether shaping is the whole problem.
   - A terminal correction: discount inventory toward zero as `day` approaches
     `SEASON_DAYS`, so the evaluator prices the thing the game actually scores.

   If this lands, `max_candidates` should rise again and the cap becomes
   unnecessary. Until it does, the cap is mitigation, not repair.

2. **Re-run every comparison the bug invalidated.** `det-mean` vs `det-worst`
   (the Phase 5 question `task.md` asks), the opponent-relative objective, and
   the `--workers` sensitivity study were all measured with the truncation
   present and mean nothing. They are cheap to redo now that results reproduce:
   two independent 100-seed runs returned the identical record.

3. **Make the rollout policy fast and good.** Unchanged in substance and still
   real: `FastPolicy` is ~5x cheaper than the baseline but plays far worse
   (~6,500 vs ~45,000), so it is not trustworthy for rollouts. A policy that is
   both would multiply search breadth - but note this is now *less* urgent than
   it looked, because breadth is currently harmful, not scarce. Fix item 1 first
   or more breadth just buys more bad decisions.

4. **Search more than one day ahead.** Melon takes 13 days to mature; a two-day
   rollout cannot see that. Closely related to item 1 - a longer horizon is one
   of the two ways to stop the evaluator mispricing the end of the season.

5. **Download real replays** (`docs/REPLAYS.md`) and replace the synthetic
   opponent pool, which is currently all variations of our own play and so
   assumes everyone farms the way we do.

6. **Tune the crop mix against market absorption.** Rough estimate of what the
   town absorbs over a season at ~base price: wheat ~525, strawberry ~426,
   carrot ~327, milk ~327, egg/wool/tomato ~228 each, melon ~30. Production
   beyond that sells into a falling price. The agent discovers this implicitly
   through marginal pricing; making it explicit should help.

7. **Opponent-aware market timing.** Both players share one market and their
   dumping is visible in `market.inventory`. Selling just *before* a predicted
   opponent dump is worth real money and nothing models it.

The 19 Aug pass audited the codebase against `task.md`. This one added the axis
that had never been checked: **what Kaggle actually publishes about the
competition**. That turned out to invalidate the statistic every result in this
file had been ranked by, and to falsify one of the previous pass's own findings.

### 1. The ladder does not rate on coin margin

From the competition's getting-started page:

> "Winning the match [...] increases your skill rating, while losing decreases
> it. [...] **The actual coin difference in a match does not affect the rating
> change - only the win, loss, or tie outcome matters.**"

and the final standings come from a **Bradley-Terry tournament** over those
outcomes. `task.md` describes a game's score as the final coin balance, which is
true of the *game* but not of the *ladder*.

Every verdict in this project was a paired t-test on coin margin. That is the
wrong statistic: an agent winning 92 games of 100 by a nose outranks one winning
86 by a landslide. `src/evaluate.py` now leads with **win rate and a Wilson
interval**, compares agents with an exact **McNemar** sign test, and reports coin
margin underneath as effect size. Details and citations in
[COMPETITION.md](COMPETITION.md).

### 2. Correction: the agents are *not* reproducible run-to-run

The 19 Aug pass recorded, as finding 6, that "both agents are reproducible
run-to-run - checked rather than assumed". **That is wrong.** It was checked on a
single seed on an idle machine, where the search budget happens not to bind.

Three runs of the shipped agent over the same 100 seeds, same `--workers 8`:

| run | record | win% | mean bank | margin |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| matrix run, 19 Aug | 86W-0T-14L | 86% | 41,064 | +8,903 |
| standalone, 19 Aug | 82W-0T-18L | 82% | 41,204 | +8,761 |
| run A, 23 Aug | 84W-0T-16L | 84% | 41,099 | +8,901 |

Pairwise, those runs of the **identical agent** disagree on **18-22 seeds out of
100** (McNemar p = 0.52-0.82, i.e. unbiased noise, not drift).

The cause is the wall-clock cutoff: how many candidates get scored before the
deadline depends on machine load, so a different macro-action gets committed.
This is now pinned by `test_search_is_deterministic_when_the_budget_is_never_binding`,
which gives the search a budget it cannot exhaust and asserts two independent
agents choose identically on every decision. It passes - so the cutoff is the
*only* source, and `task.md`'s "deterministic seeding everywhere" holds for
everything except the anytime search.

### 3. The noise floor is larger than any difference being measured

Putting §2 next to the variant comparisons is the most useful thing this pass
produced:

| comparison | seeds where the two disagree |
|---|---:|
| identical agent, two runs | **18 - 22** |
| det-worst vs mcts | 14 |
| det-mean vs mcts | 11 |
| relative vs absolute objective | 17 |
| det-worst vs det-mean | 9 |

**Every variant difference sits below the harness's own noise floor.** One run
per variant cannot resolve any of them, and the paired design does not help:
pairing cancels seed randomness, not timing randomness. Chasing this with more
episodes is the expensive fix; making the budget deterministic - counting
rollouts instead of milliseconds - is the cheap one, and is now the top item in
"what I would do next".

| det-mean | 91W-0T-9L | 91% | [83.8%, 95.2%] | 41,939 | WINS |
| det-worst | 92W-0T-8L | 92% | [85.0%, 95.9%] | 41,399 | WINS |

Pairwise McNemar: det-worst vs mcts **p = 0.18**, det-mean vs mcts **p = 0.23**,
det-worst vs det-mean **p = 1.00**. Every search variant beats `baseline`
(p < 0.0001); none beats another.

So the Phase 5 question `task.md` asks - which aggregation scores better -
remains **unresolved**, and now for a better-understood reason than "not enough
episodes": see §3. `mcts` stays shipped because nothing has been shown to beat
it, not because it was shown to be best.

Note also that the `baseline` self-play row reads 50.5% only after the tie fix in
§5. It had been reported as 47%, which looks like an agent losing to itself.

### 5. Three statistics bugs, all in the direction of overclaiming

1. **Ties counted as losses.** `bank > opponent_bank` with no tie branch. Seven
   of 100 `baseline` self-play seeds end level; scoring them as losses dragged a
   dead-even result to 47%. Ties are now half a win, per Bradley-Terry.
2. **`mean_turn_time` accumulated with `max()`** - it reported the worst
   episode's mean under a label that said "mean".
3. **`_student_t_critical` rounded degrees of freedom up**, returning t(12) for
   df=11. Smaller critical value, narrower interval, more willing to call noise
   an improvement. It now rounds down.

All three are covered by `tests/test_statistics.py` (25 tests).

### 6. The self-play validation episode was never tested

Kaggle runs a validation episode - the agent against a copy of itself - before
admitting a submission to the ladder, and a failure there rejects it outright.
Nothing local had ever run `mcts` against `mcts`. It is also the only
configuration where *both* seats search.

`tools/verify_submission.py` now has `check_self_play()` as a **blocker**. It
loads the bundle twice under different module names so each seat gets its own
agent instance, as two Kaggle processes would. Result: 720 steps, both seats
DONE, **max turn 446 ms** - comfortable, and measured under the load that
actually matters rather than against a cheap built-in.

### 7. Opponent-relative objective: tested, rejected

The ladder pays for beating the opponent, and the opponent's bank is public and
exact in every observation - yet `_value()` maximised our own net worth and
ignored them entirely. Added `objective="absolute"|"relative"`; relative scores
`fast_net_worth(me) - bank_of(opponent)`. Their **bank** rather than their net
worth, deliberately: their money is exact where their shed is a guess, and
unsold inventory scores zero at season end anyway.

Measured head to head, same 100 seeds, back to back:

| objective | record | win% | 95% CI | mean bank | margin | max turn |
|---|---:|---:|---|---:|---:|---:|
| absolute (incumbent) | 84W-0T-16L | 84% | [75.6%, 89.9%] | 41,099 | +8,901 | 523 ms |
| relative (new) | 87W-0T-13L | 87% | [79.0%, 92.2%] | 41,046 | +9,411 | 640 ms |

McNemar: 10 seeds relative-only, 7 absolute-only, **p = 0.63**. Not a difference;
17 discordant seeds is inside the same-agent noise floor from §3. Max turn also

**Not shipped.** The flag stays, defaulted to `absolute`, so the experiment is
repeatable without re-deriving it. This was committed to in advance: ship only on
a win by win rate.

## Third verification pass (30 Aug 2026) - the submission dialog

The 23 Aug pass read the competition's *published pages*. This one read the
**submission dialog itself**, which states a binding rule those pages do not,
and which our own docs had recorded wrongly.

### 1. Kaggle binds a bare `.py` by position, not by name

Verbatim from the upload panel:

> "Your submission should be a python file with the **last 'def'** accepting an
> observation and returning an action. You can also upload multiple files in a
> **zip/gz/7z** archive with a `main.py` at the top level."

Two rules, one per upload shape. `docs/COMPETITION.md` recorded only the second
one - *"must have a `main.py` at the root with an `agent` function"* - and
applied it to the single-file case, where it does not hold. It also claimed
`.tar.gz` was preferred over `.zip`; the dialog accepts zip/gz/7z and never
mentions tar.

**Why this was live risk rather than pedantry.** `submission/main.py` is a
generated flattening of six modules: 2,848 lines, ~45 top-level `def`s. `agent`
is last only because `build_submission` appends `FOOTER` last. Nothing enforced
that. Adding a module to `MODULES` after the footer, or a helper below `agent`,
would bind the wrong function and forfeit every episode - and **no existing gate
could have caught it**, because `verify_submission` tested `hasattr(module,
"agent")`, which stays true while Kaggle runs something else.

It was correct on inspection. It was correct by luck.

### 2. The gate, and proving the gate works

`check_entry_point_contract()` in `tools/verify_submission.py`, a blocker, run
before anything executes the bundle. Pure AST: the last top-level `def` is
`agent`, it is also the last statement in the module, and every parameter past
the first has a default (the dialog says "accepting an observation", singular,
so `agent(obs)` must work as well as `agent(obs, config)`).

`tests/test_submission_contract.py` locks it, and one of those six tests earns
its place specifically: it appends a trailing `def` to a copy of the real bundle
and **requires the checker to reject it**. A guard that has never been observed
to fail is indistinguishable from one that cannot fail. This pass added the
check and the proof that it bites.

### 3. Two artifacts, because the two rules fail differently

`build_submission` now also writes `submission/kaggriculture.zip` - the same
bytes as `main.py` at the archive's top level, 30 KB compressed from 109 KB.

The point is not redundancy, it is that the zip is bound **by filename**, so it
is structurally immune to the positional rule above. If Kaggle ever fails to
find the entry point in the `.py`, the zip is the fallback that cannot fail that
way. `ZipInfo.date_time` is pinned to a fixed epoch, or the archive's bytes
would change on every rebuild and the "reproducible from src/" property would
hold for the `.py` and quietly not for the `.zip`.

`.zip` rather than `.gz`: a bare gzip stream carries one nameless blob, so
"main.py at the top level" cannot be expressed in it at all.

### 4. A timing margin, stated rather than implied

The timing gate blocked only at the full 1,000 ms `actTimeout`. Worst turn here
is ~430 ms, which sounds comfortable until you notice the measurement was taken
on a 22-core desktop and Kaggle's runner is not this machine.

`SAFE_TURN_FRACTION = 0.70` now raises a **warning** above 700 ms in both the
episode and self-play checks. A warning, not a blocker: it is a judgement about
hardware we cannot measure, not a rule the submission breaks.

### 5. A footgun worth writing down

Bare `python` on this box resolves to `AppData/Local/hermes/hermes-agent/venv`,
which has no `kaggle_environments` - seven tests fail with `ModuleNotFoundError`
for that reason alone, which looks exactly like a real regression. **Use
`./.venv/Scripts/python.exe`.** Recorded at the top of `docs/SUBMISSION.md`
because that is where someone in a hurry will be looking.

### 6. Documentation

- `docs/COMPETITION.md` section 2 rewritten: both binding rules, the real
  accepted formats, the three dialog tabs, and the quota as the dialog states it.
  Also sharpened section 4 - "latest 2 tracked" is positional, not "best 2", so
  two careless uploads evict a good agent from the tracked pair.
- `docs/SUBMISSION.md` is new: the browser upload runbook, which file to drag,
  what to write in the description, what Kaggle does next, and a failure table.

## Fourth pass (30 Aug 2026) - the agent was configured to lose

This pass set out to verify the submission against the upload dialog. It found
the entry-point rule (see above), and then, while running the evaluation the way
`task.md` asks for, it found that **every performance number this project has
ever reported was an artifact**, and that the shipped configuration was close to
the worst available.

### 1. The bug: strategy was a function of CPU load

`_search` truncated its candidate list by measured machine speed:

```python
affordable = int(self.budget_s / self._est_rollout_s(horizon0))
if affordable < len(candidates):
    candidates = candidates[:max(2, affordable)]
```

The intent was reasonable - do not propose more options than the budget can
score, because a partial sweep is discarded. The consequence was not: **how many
strategies the agent considered depended on how busy the box was that second.**

That would be tolerable if the candidate count barely mattered. It is, in fact,
the single most important parameter in the system.

### 2. The sweep - `max_candidates` decides everything

100 identical seeds against `baseline`, budget raised to 20 s so the deadline can
never bind, so each row is a clean measurement of *one* candidate count:

| max_candidates | record | win rate | note |
|---:|---|---:|---|
| 1 | 41W-6T-37L | 52.4% | only "auto" - this **is** the plain heuristic |
| **2** | 83W-0T-9L | **90.2%** | auto + one crop choice |
| **3** | 88W-0T-12L | **88.0%** | |
| 4 | 77W-0T-23L | 77.0% | |
| 5 | 30W-0T-70L | **30.0%** | **the shipped default** |
| 6 | 1W-0T-99L | **1.0%** | adds `sell-patient` |

Losing 99 of 100 is not a mistuned parameter. It is an evaluator that ranks
strategies backwards, and every extra option handed to it is another way to pick
a bad one.

### 3. Why the historical numbers were 84-86%

Because a loaded machine truncated to 2 candidates - the *best* setting - and an
idle one scored all 5. The three earlier verification passes each measured CPU
load and reported it as skill. The 19 Aug matrix (86%), the 23 Aug standalone
(84%), the "82/84/86% noise band", the det-mean/det-worst comparison, the
opponent-relative objective test: all of them, invalid.

The "18-22 seeds flip between identical runs" finding from 23 Aug was real, and
was the visible tip of this. It was written up as irreducible timing noise and
filed as future work, when it was a reproducible bug with a one-line cause.

### 4. The root cause underneath: the value function rewards hoarding

`_value` scores rollouts with `fast_net_worth`, which counts **unsold inventory
as wealth**. The rollout horizon is 2 days; the season is 30. So "hold stock,
prices may rise" always scores well inside the window - and unsold inventory is
worth **zero coins** at season end.

`_value`'s own docstring has this backwards:

> "Raw cash over three days would score every seed, hand and animal as a pure
> loss and drive the agent to hoard, which is precisely the failure `task.md`
> warns about."

Shaping does not prevent the hoarding failure here; it produces it. That is why
candidate 6 - `sell-patient`, which literally raises the selling threshold - is
catastrophic, and why `det-mean` (which runs *more* of this evaluator) scored 29%
in an earlier matrix.

### 5. Fixes applied

- **The truncation is deleted.** `max_candidates` is now the only thing that
  decides the candidate set. The wall-clock deadline remains purely as an
  `actTimeout` safety net; if it ever binds, the partial sweep is discarded and
  the agent falls back to the heuristic - a safe and recorded degradation rather
  than a silent change of strategy.
- `tests/test_agents.py::test_candidate_set_does_not_depend_on_machine_speed` -
  runs the search with a 60 s budget and a 1 ms budget and requires the same
  candidate set from both. The pre-existing determinism test only covered the
  non-binding regime, which is why it passed throughout.
- `tests/test_submission_contract.py::test_shipped_config_matches_the_config_we_evaluate`
  - the FOOTER in `build_submission.py` constructs `MCTSAgent` with **explicit
  kwargs** while `--agent mcts` uses **class defaults**. Nothing kept them in
  step, so the benchmark could describe an agent that is not the one uploaded.
  With a 90%-to-1% spread across this parameter, that gap matters more than any
  other check in the repo.

### 6. Confirmed reproducible

`max_candidates=5` returned `30W-0T-70L` in two independent runs - identical
record, identical seeds, different invocations. That is the first exact
reproduction this project has produced, and it is the evidence the fix works.

### 7. Resolution

`max_candidates=2` shipped, chosen by measurement at the real 450 ms budget:

| config | record | win rate | 95% CI | mean turn | max turn |
|---|---|---:|---|---:|---:|
| **2** | **93W-0T-7L** | **93.0%** | [86.3%, 96.6%] | **4.5 ms** | **326 ms** |
| 3 | 89W-0T-11L | 89.0% | [81.4%, 93.7%] | 9.2 ms | 470 ms |

2 wins on both axes - higher win rate *and* half the turn cost, which buys back
`actTimeout` headroom. Both rows completed 100/100 episodes.

Set in **three** places, because they could previously drift apart:
`src/mcts_agent.py` (class default), `src/determinization.py` (subclass default,
which would otherwise reintroduce 5), and the `FOOTER` in
`tools/build_submission.py` (what actually ships). The last is now pinned by
`test_shipped_config_matches_the_config_we_evaluate`.

**Reproducibility achieved, with one honest caveat.** On an idle box the search
takes exactly 2.0 rollouts per decision, 0% timed out, 156 ms of its 450 ms
budget - fully deterministic. Two independent 100-seed runs returned the
identical record `93W-0T-7L`.

Under 4-way parallel load, 18 of 100 per-seed banks still differed between those
two runs while the record stayed identical. The cause is the surviving deadline
pre-check (`if now + est_rollout > deadline: break`), which can fire when a
worker is descheduled. That is *not* worth removing: some wall-clock guard is
mandatory against a 1 s `actTimeout`, and full bit-determinism under arbitrary
load is incompatible with a hard real-time deadline. What mattered - the
*strategy* changing with machine speed - is gone and pinned by test.

| `tools.verify_submission` | **READY TO SUBMIT** |

### 9. What is still open

1. **The value function.** `_value` counts unsold inventory as wealth over a
   2-day horizon in a 30-day season, so it rewards hoarding. Capping candidates
   at 2 avoids handing it the options it most misjudges; it does not fix the
   misjudgement. A terminal correction (value inventory at zero near season end,
   or lengthen the horizon) is the real repair, and would likely make *more*
   search helpful again rather than harmful.
2. **`shaped_reward=False` was never measured.** The run was started and killed.
   It is the cheapest test of item 1.
3. **Determinization is unevaluated under the fix.** `det-mean`/`det-worst` were
   only ever measured with the truncation bug present, so those comparisons - and
   the Phase 5 question `task.md` asks - remain genuinely unanswered.

## Fifth pass (31 Aug 2026) - closing the open items

The fourth pass left three things open: the evaluator, determinization's missing
test coverage, and Phase 5. All three are now closed - two with answers, one with
a negative result that is worth as much.

### 1. The evaluator: diagnosis confirmed, repair does not ship

The fourth pass argued that `_value` pricing shed stock at full market rate makes
holding look free, and that this is why win rate collapses as more candidates are
offered. Three probes using the *existing* flags, n=100 each:

| max_candidates | shaped (`fast_net_worth`) | unshaped (raw bank) |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 2 | **93%** | **14%** |
| 5 | 30% | **66%** |

A clean interaction, and both halves of the old docstring turn out to be half
right. Shaping **is** required at low candidate counts - raw bank rates every
seed, hand and animal as a pure loss, and the agent collapses to 14%. Shaping is
also what kills high candidate counts. Neither pure option is correct.

So `valuation = "market" | "liquidation"` was added: `liquidation` prices
sellable stock through `sell_revenue`, which re-quotes each unit as inventory
rises, while standing crops, animals, seeds and land keep their shaped
treatment. Measured, n=100:

| max_candidates | market | liquidation |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 2 | **93%** | 92% |
| 3 | 89% | 86% |
| 5 | 30% | **84%** |
| 6 | 1% | 2% |

**The diagnosis was right.** At 5 candidates, liquidation pricing is worth
**+54 points** (30% -> 84%), which is about as direct a confirmation of a
mechanism as this project has produced.

**It still does not ship.** At the shipped `max_candidates=2` it scores 92%
against the incumbent's 93% - McNemar **p = 1.0000**, differing on exactly one
seed. Indistinguishable, and not a win. The ship rule was fixed in advance
precisely so a satisfying mechanism result could not be talked into a release,
and this is the case it was written for. The flag stays, defaulted to `market`.

### 2. Horizon is a second, separate cause

`max_candidates=6` stays catastrophic under liquidation pricing - **2%**, versus
1% before. So `sell-patient` is not harmful only because stock was mispriced. A
**2-day rollout cannot see the consequence of not selling** across a 30-day
season however accurately it values the stock. Horizon and valuation are
independent defects, and only the second has been addressed. This is now
evidence rather than conjecture, and it is why "search more than one day ahead"
is item 4 in the next-steps list rather than a nice-to-have.

### 3. Phase 5, answered

`task.md` asks which aggregation scores better. n=100, identical seeds:

Pairwise McNemar between all three search agents: **0 discordant seeds,

That settles it for the shipped agent, and it justifies shipping plain `mcts`:

## Sixth pass (8 Sep 2026) - external review, and a negative result

An external review raised nine issues. Five were real, one was factually wrong,
the rest already documented. This pass fixed three and measured the big one,
which **did not ship**.

### 1. The review's best finding was real, and the model is now exact

`daily_town_drain` modelled demand as `shops_unlocked_at(day)` (a count) x
`SHOP_PULL_RATE` (averaged over all 8 shop types), while the environment consumes
from the **realized list**, at double rate for single-product shops:

```python
# kaggriculture.py:736-744
for shop_name in town["unlocked_shops"]:      # a LIST, duplicates allowed
    products = SHOPS[shop_name]
    multiplier = 2 if len(products) == 1 else 1
```

`View.unlocked_shops` was already parsed - the ground truth was in hand and
unused. `daily_town_drain(item, day, unlocked_shops=None)` now prices observed
instances exactly and falls back to the average only for instances not yet drawn.

Validated against the environment rather than against itself: both seats play
`pass`, so every market-inventory change is town consumption, and the observed
one-day delta is directly comparable.

| model | mean abs error vs ground truth | worst case |
|---|---:|---:|
| averaged (old) | **4.22 units/day** | **19x** (predicted 19, truth was 1) |
| realized (new) | **0.00** | exact |

609 day/item samples across three seeds. The model is right.

### 2. And it made the agent worse

Ship rule, fixed in advance: improve mean bank vs `starter` **and** not regress
win rate vs `baseline`. Both measured at n=100 on identical seeds.

| metric | OFF (incumbent) | ON | verdict |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| mean bank vs `starter` | **51,385** | 49,623 | **-1,762**, 95% CI [-3,126, -398], t=-2.58 |
| win rate vs `baseline` | **93W-0T-7L** | 91W-0T-9L | p=0.6875, indistinguishable |

The economy metric - the one the fix targets - **significantly regressed**, with
the interval excluding zero. So a strictly more accurate component made the
system worse, and the flag stays defaulted off.

`Params.REALIZED_SHOP_DEMAND` remains, so the experiment is repeatable without
re-deriving it, and `daily_town_drain(..., unlocked_shops=...)` stays because it
is *correct* and the parity test is worth having regardless.

### 3. Why - partially understood, and worth saying so

The obvious hypothesis was concentration: a sharper demand signal makes the agent
pile into whatever the open shops buy, flooding that market because
`sell_revenue` walks our own price down. **The data does not support it.**
Crop-mix concentration is unchanged (HHI 0.46 -> 0.48), and on two of three
sampled seeds the mid-season mix is *identical* under both flags while the final
banks differ - so the divergence is not mainly crop choice.

What is visible: on seed 11 the realized model swapped 30 WHEAT for 32 TOMATO and
lost 11k. `project_inventory` is documented as "deliberately optimistic about
scarcity - it does not model the opponent dumping supply", and it does not model
*our own* future supply either. A sharper demand estimate therefore feeds a
projection that already over-believes scarcity, and pushes production toward a
thinner market (wheat absorbs ~525 units a season, tomato ~228).

That is a hypothesis, not a demonstration. The honest summary is that the demand
model is now exact and the component consuming it cannot yet exploit the extra
accuracy.

**The general lesson, which is the reason this is written up at all:** making one
component strictly more accurate is not automatically an improvement. It is only
an improvement if everything downstream can use the sharper signal. Here the
projection cannot, so better inputs produced worse decisions - and the only
reason we know is that the ship rule was fixed before the measurement.

### 4. Other review items

- **Vacuous assertion** (`test_fidelity.py:148`): `assert ... or True` could never
  fail. Replaced with a snapshot comparison taken before the clone is mutated,
  which is correct at any step - and verified to fail when the town dict is
  deliberately aliased, so it is not vacuous a second time.
- **Seat-0-only evaluation**: real. `run_episode` gained `time_seat` and
  `evaluate` gained `--seats {a,b,both}` with a per-seat breakdown, so pooling
  cannot hide a seat asymmetry. Measured: **93% from seat 0, 87% from seat 1** -
  a modest effect that changes no conclusion.
- **`NoisyBaselineAgent(seed=0)`**: its docstring claimed per-episode jitter it
  does not do from the registry. Docstring corrected; behaviour left alone, since
  it sits in determinization's opponent pool where it *does* get a varying seed.
- **240-step fidelity test**: `tools/bench_sim.py` compares the full 719 as a
  gate; the narrower test scope is now a stated choice.

### 5. The claim that was wrong

> "Candidate plans alter the number and ordering of random calls, so different
> candidates are compared under candidate-dependent futures."

False. The environment re-seeds daily -
